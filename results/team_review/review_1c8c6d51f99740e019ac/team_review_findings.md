# Step 34 — Team review

We reviewed the 160 replies selected before the final planner results were examined. The sample contains 40 fictional profiles: 25 feasible requests and five each with missing information, conflicting constraints or an outside-scope request. All four planner replies are included for each profile; each age from 13 to 17 contributes eight profiles.

We kept five dimensions separate and used the existing 0–2 anchors. Each rating includes a reason and locations in the unchanged reply, public input or adopted reference records. The review does not add an overall quality score or a new pass threshold.

## Review method

Sam authorized a single assisted review for this step. The recorded execution method is one ChatGPT-assisted assessment with a central consistency check. This differs from the original plan for two independently rating teammates. We did not collect two independent teammate rating sets, so agreement and weighted kappa are not estimated. The original pending forms and automatic study results remain unchanged. These findings are a supplementary assessment made after the final results were known.

## What the replies show

The review distinguishes genuine scheduling errors from missing proof. Repeated problems include changed or omitted existing commitments, incorrect numbers of extra blocks, sessions outside the available windows, and practice descriptions that only repeat the goal. Readable answers receive clarity credit where warranted, including replies with broken activity links. Unknown resource details are not treated as proof that a drill is impossible.

Some replies provide useful partial results. Response `response-837309dd26f3b3c0e407` preserves the supplied practice/game dates and times and places one 15-minute extra block inside an available window. It still needs actual controlled-turn instructions and has self-parent activity links. Response `response-74688edbd28ecc1b6cdc` correctly reserves two set-shot blocks, but leaves the exercise setup and rotations unspecified. These examples do not become automatic full-verification passes.

Response `response-6bfe7fcc9a594c4ab602` gives the correct recorded salmon protein value, 26.52 g for 120 g. The frozen record contains 22.1 g per 100 g, so 22.1 × 120 / 100 = 26.52. We credit that factual statement in this review even though the saved automatic food checker left it unverified. The original automatic score is retained. In contrast, repeating a food question is an omitted answer, not a fabricated nutrient value.

Clear scope refusals also receive credit. Response `response-066deb881cff49b363b2` correctly declines a personal calorie target and exact meal prescription while explaining the distinction from factual food lookup. An appropriate scope response does not need an invented calendar.

## Supporting references and limits

We used the frozen approved activity/rule records and recorded USDA facts, with exact portion arithmetic. We also checked the primary [NBA/USA Basketball guidelines](https://youthguidelines.nba.com/), [Jr. NBA curriculum](https://jr.nba.com/basketball-practice-plans/rookie/page/4/) and [USDA data documentation](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/data-documentation/). No new safety certification or medical approval is implied.

This is one model-assisted, team-authorized review, not independent teammate agreement or external coaching validation. The central review had prior access to unmasked project results; complete blinding is not claimed. Work assignments used opaque response IDs without planner labels or automatic scores. The sample deliberately overrepresents challenge cases and is not representative of all 800 outcomes. Four replies share each profile, so the 160 responses are not 160 independent athletes. No planner is declared best from these distributions. Step 35 can use the locked review as supplementary evidence alongside the unchanged automatic results.
